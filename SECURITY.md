# Security

Obol Ledger keeps books for real businesses, alongside a public demo anyone can
read. Reports are welcome.

## Reporting

Open a [private security advisory](https://github.com/dnakhoa/obol-ledger/security/advisories/new).
Please do not open a public issue for anything exploitable.

I will acknowledge within a few days. There is no bounty.

## What is in scope

Anything that breaks an invariant the project claims to hold:

- An entry that commits without summing to zero
- Reading or writing another tenant's rows, documents or e-invoices
- Writing to any ledger without being a member who may write to it — the
  demo included
- A balance that disagrees with the postings behind it
- An e-invoice number that skips or repeats within a series
- Forging a webhook signature, or making the dispatcher reach a private address
- Recovering an API key or webhook secret from anything the application
  returns, renders or logs
- Running script in the application's origin: through an upload, an export,
  an entry description, or anything else a user can type

## How the application defends itself

So a report can say which layer failed:

- **Tenancy** is enforced by Postgres row-level security, under a role that
  cannot bypass it; the application sets the tenant per transaction.
  `/api/v1/health` reports whether the running connection is subject to it.
- **Writes** need a signed-in member with a writing role, or an API key. A
  signed-out visitor reads the demo and can change nothing.
- **Pages** are served with a per-request nonce Content-Security-Policy, so
  only the application's own scripts run, plus HSTS, `frame-ancestors 'none'`
  and `nosniff`.
- **Uploads** are typed by their content, not their name: PDF, PNG, JPEG,
  WebP, or plain XML with no DOCTYPE and nothing from SVG or XHTML. They are
  served as downloads or under a sandboxing policy of their own.
- **Exports** neutralise spreadsheet formulas and quote anything a
  spreadsheet might split on.
- **Rate limits** are counted in Postgres, shared by every instance, and
  keyed on the address the nearest proxy saw.
- **Webhook targets** must be HTTPS to a public address, checked for the
  literal host and for every address it resolves to, with IPv4 addresses
  written as IPv6 unwrapped first. Redirects are not followed.

## Known limits

Documented rather than hidden:

- **DNS rebinding** against a webhook target. The resolved address is
  checked, but pinning it into the connection is not something Node's
  `fetch` exposes without another dependency.
- **Webhook signing secrets are stored in plaintext**, readable by the
  database owner. They are shown to the subscriber once and never returned
  again.
- **Sign-in rate limiting** inside the authentication library is
  per-instance.
- **Sample ledgers are not yet purged.** Their creation is capped per
  address and per day, and their uploads per ledger.
- **Uploads are not virus-scanned.** They are typed, size-limited and never
  executed by the application; scan them before opening them elsewhere.

## Out of scope

- Reading the demo tenant's data. It is published on purpose.
- Anything requiring physical access, a compromised developer machine, or
  social engineering.
- Reports generated by a scanner with no demonstration of impact, and findings
  about dependencies with no reachable path from this code.
